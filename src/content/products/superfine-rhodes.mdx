## Positioning

A **new Katambora-type diploid Rhodes grass**, bred in Australia from the market-leading Tolgar Rhodes. Retains Tolgar's strengths while delivering finer-textured growth, higher leaf-to-stem ratio, and later, more uniform flowering — for hay, grazing, reclamation, and soil conservation.

## Key features

- Significantly later flowering than Tolgar Rhodes
- Greater synchronization and uniformity of flowering
- Later flowering than Katambora and synthetic Rhodes grasses
- Higher leaf-to-stem ratio and finer stem vs Tolgar Rhodes
- Highly suited to hay production — exceptional hay quality
- Well suited to grazing, reclamation, soil conservation
- Highly palatable — greater feed utilization, less stem
- Higher salt tolerance than Katambora Rhodes
- Nematode-resistant — ideal rotational option
- Moderate drought, frost, cool-season, and acid tolerance

## Product narrative

Bred in Australia from the market-leading Tolgar Rhodes, retaining its strengths while improving forage quality. Thinner leaf blades produce finer-textured vegetative growth with reduced stem. Enhanced multiple tillering yields a higher leaf-to-stem ratio → greater consumable dry matter than Tolgar Rhodes and other Katambora Rhodes grasses. Conditioning and dry-down significantly improved → less stem, more dry-down rate, faster utilization of leaf dry-down gains. Maturity evenness yields higher protein feed later in the season at higher utilization rates. Exceptional persistence and recovery after grazing or cutting. Later flowering than Tolgar Rhodes maintains feed quality longer.

## Agronomy detail

Suited to tropical, sub-tropical, and (with conditions) temperate zones. Wide soil adaptation: infertile sands to fertile loams to Brigalow clays. Requires adequate nutrition and some form of rotational grazing for medium-to-long-term persistence. Diploid Rhodes establishes and persists well even at low fertility, with higher drought tolerance and faster growth than tetraploid Rhodes types. Can struggle on heavy cracking clays. Moderate frost tolerance, better than tetraploid Rhodes.
